## Background

When one eye is impaired, the cortical response linked to that eye is reduced or absent. fMRI and EEG capture the response driven by the healthy eye, and this project explores whether those signals carry enough information for a generative model to estimate the missing response.

## Research questions

- Can generative models estimate the missing V1 response from healthy-eye fMRI and EEG signals?
- How can the reconstructed activity be validated, and how can its uncertainty be reported?
- Which signal features does the model rely on, and are they neuroscientifically meaningful?

## Objectives

- Develop a multimodal (fMRI and EEG) generative framework for neural activity reconstruction
- Evaluate reconstruction quality and uncertainty against appropriate benchmarks
- Analyse the interpretability of the learned representations

## Approach

Generative modelling of multimodal neural signals, combined with uncertainty estimation and explainability analysis.

## Expected contribution

A validated, uncertainty-aware framework for estimating missing cortical activity, with relevance to the study of monocular vision loss.

## Related experience

- Explainable and calibrated clinical ML (manuscript under review)
- Time-series and bio-signal analysis
